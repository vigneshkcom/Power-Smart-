Smoke Alarm Certificate of Compliance
About this certificate

A Smoke Alarm ‘Certificate of Compliance’ can only be issued when the Electrician certifies the entire dwelling is compliant at the time of installation/inspection.

If a Smoke Alarm has been tampered with, or the premises undergoes construction changes after the certificate is issued, the premises ‘may’ become incompliant.

Queensland’s Smoke Alarm regulations: www.qfes.qld.gov.au/smokealarms
Maintenance
1 Read the manufacturer instructions.
2 Once a month, test the alarm by pressing the test button.
3 Keep alarms clean. Dust and debris can interfere with their operation, so vacuum over and around them regularly. Failure to maintain them may lead to unwanted activations.
4 Smoke Alarms must never be painted.
5 If cooking smoke sets off the alarm, do not disable it. Turn on the range fan, open a window or wave a towel near the alarm, or use the hush button.
6 If activations while cooking persist, relocate the alarm or change the range fan to exhaust externally.
What causes false activations of Smoke Alarms

In most cases false activations are not false. Common triggers are cigarette smoke, vaping, burnt toast, smoke entering from outside, excessive humidity, or dust passing the sensor. Identify the activated alarm, then inspect that room for the cause.

If hardwired interconnected photoelectric alarms activate with no identifiable cause, the issue could be the power supply. In some regional or remote areas of Queensland, fluctuations in power supply (‘dirty power’) from the network, electronic equipment or solar can cause false activations. A filter installed on the switchboard reduces the impact.
